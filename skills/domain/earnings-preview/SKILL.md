# Earnings Preview

Use this skill before a company reports earnings to define what matters, what
would change the view, and what the research team should collect after release.

## Boundaries

- Do not invent consensus, whisper numbers, implied moves, or historical
  reactions.
- If current data is needed, fetch or cite it with date and source.
- Do not default to a trading recommendation.
- Express scenarios, thresholds, evidence, and follow-up actions.

## Workflow

### Identify The Setup

- Company and ticker.
- Reporting quarter and fiscal period.
- Expected report date and time.
- Source and source date for timing.
- User concern or thesis pillar affected.

### Establish Expectations

Record expectation data with source/date:

- Revenue.
- EPS.
- Guidance.
- Segment metrics.
- Margin, capex, free cash flow, or balance-sheet items when relevant.
- Industry/company-specific KPIs.

If data is unavailable, mark the gap as fetchable, computable,
provider-required, or user-required.

### Define Key Questions

Pick the three to five questions most likely to change the view.

### Scenario Framework

Use scenarios as evidence plans, not price predictions:

- Scenario.
- Evidence needed.
- Thesis impact.
- Metric threshold.
- Follow-up.

Scenario thresholds must have formulas or sources when numeric.

### Post-Earnings Task List

Define what the team will do after release:

- Parse reported metrics.
- Compare actuals to expectations.
- Update affected thesis pillars.
- Update assumptions if needed.
- Recompute valuation snapshot if relevant.
- Create follow-up questions for transcript or call.

## Quality Gates

- Every expectation has source and date.
- Every key metric is company/industry appropriate.
- Scenario thresholds are concrete and auditable.
- Missing data is routed.
- Preview links to thesis, catalyst, valuation, or watch item.
- Post-earnings follow-up is explicit.
